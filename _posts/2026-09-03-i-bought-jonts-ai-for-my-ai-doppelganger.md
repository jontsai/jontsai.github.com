I did a very normal thing: I bought [`jonts.ai`](https://www.jonts.ai) for 2 years for $179.96 so my AI alter ego could have a proper home.

![Purchase summary showing jonts.ai domain registration for 2 years at $179.96](/img/posts/2026-09-03-jonts-ai/purchase-summary.png)

The site is intentionally tiny:

[https://www.jonts.ai](https://www.jonts.ai)

![Screenshot of the jonts.ai landing page with a colored ASCII-art portrait](/img/posts/2026-09-03-jonts-ai/site-preview.png)

It says, simply:

> jonts.ai is the home of Jonathan Tsai's personal AI assistant / döppelgänger. For the human, see [jontsai.com](https://jontsai.com).

The project now lives under its own GitHub handle:

[https://github.com/jonts-ai](https://github.com/jonts-ai)

The fun part is the portrait.

I used my GitHub avatar as the source image, then generated a colored ASCII-art rendering where each text glyph acts like a pixel. Up close, it is made of little characters. From a distance, it resolves back into a face. That felt like the right aesthetic for an AI alter ego: half identity, half terminal output.

There is no official pronunciation for `jonts.ai` yet. One possible rendition is "Jon T's AI," which is ridiculous enough that it might stick.

This is mostly whimsical, but it also marks something real: I am increasingly building with AI agents as persistent collaborators, not just one-off tools. Giving the assistant a public home, a GitHub handle, and a tiny bit of personality makes the whole thing feel more concrete.

For the AI: [https://www.jonts.ai](https://www.jonts.ai)  
For the human: [https://jontsai.com](https://jontsai.com)
